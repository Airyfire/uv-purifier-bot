# Experiment 2: Centralized AC vs HEPA Purifier

**Status:** ⬜ Planned

## Research Question
Does centralized AC clean polluted air as effectively as a dedicated HEPA purifier, and does it reduce stagnant zone disparities?

## Hypothesis
Centralized AC will circulate air more evenly across zones (reducing the gap between near and stagnant readings) but will remove fewer total particles than the HEPA purifier — because standard AC filters are not rated to capture fine particulates (PM2.5) at the same efficiency as HEPA.

## Why This Test Matters
Centralized AC is the most common "air cleaning" method people use at home. If it performs comparably to a HEPA purifier, the justification for a dedicated purifier robot weakens. If it doesn't, it strengthens the case for the robot — especially in stagnant zones that AC circulation may still miss.

## Procedure

1. **Baseline** — AC off, everything off. Record PM2.5 at all 3 sensors.
2. **Pollute** — Burn incense for 30–60 seconds. Let disperse for 1–2 minutes.
3. **Activate AC** — Turn on centralized AC. Begin recording.
4. **Record** — Log PM2.5 at all sensors. Take snapshot at t = 0 and t = 60 min (match Experiment 1 timing).

> 💡 If possible, log continuously every minute — this will show whether AC spreads particles evenly first before removing them, which a snapshot would miss.
